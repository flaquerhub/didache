Commentary on 3 John

1-2 John identified himself as "the elder"; this can refer to an older man or a leader in the Christian community, what came to be known as a bishop or priest. It is addressed to Gaius, of whom nothing else is known. The letter is largely concerned with the problem of a leader of a certain Christian community who did not recognize the Apostle's authority. (CCC 459)

3-8 John praises Gaius, who appears to be a layman, for the positive reports he had heard regarding his life, service, and example to the community. He is one who is united to Christ, walks in his light, and upholds his truth. My children: Like Paul and other Apostles and missionaries, John considers himself a spiritual father to the people he serves. (CCC 432, 2044-2046)

9-12 The local leader, Diotrephes, refused to acknowledge John's authority, harshly criticized him, and would not receive the envoys John had sent to him. John gave his endorsement to Demetrius and his teachings. He who does good is of God: If we are to carry out Christ's mission of proclaiming the Gospel, we must validate these truths by the witness of our lives. (CCC 553, 861-862, 2044, 2235)

13-15 Peace be with you: The traditional Jewish greeting, Shalom, which the Christians had adopted for themselves. (CCC 1301, 1567)
